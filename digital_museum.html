Bronze Human Head with Gold Mask

A bronze head adorned with a delicate gold mask, symbolizing divine or royal status. This artifact reflects the craftsmanship and spiritual beliefs of the ancient Shu civilization.

Bronze Sacred Tree

A tall, intricately cast bronze tree believed to represent a sacred or cosmic axis connecting heaven and earth. Its branches and decorations may symbolize mythological figures or celestial realms.

Bronze Mask with Protruding Eyes

An imposing bronze mask featuring exaggerated, protruding eyes. Thought to represent a deity or shaman figure, it illustrates the surreal aesthetic and ritual purpose of ancient ceremonial masks.

Golden Scepter

A symbol of authority and spiritual power, this finely crafted golden scepter likely belonged to a high-ranking figure. Its design and material reflect the elite status of its owner.

Ivory Artifact

A carefully preserved ivory piece, possibly used as a ritual object or ornament. Its craftsmanship reveals long-distance trade and high artistry in material culture.

Jade Zhang with Mountain Sacrifice Motif

A ritual jade blade engraved with scenes of mountain worship. This artifact embodies the importance of nature and religious ceremony in early Chinese belief systems.

Bronze Standing Figure

A life-sized bronze figure standing with poised authority. Possibly representing a priest, shaman, or ruler, it reflects both political power and spiritual significance.

Earthenware Tripod Cooking Vessel

A ceramic tripod vessel used for cooking or ritual offerings. Its functional design and cultural context highlight the daily and ceremonial life of ancient communities.

The First Bronze Chariot

A meticulously crafted bronze chariot with horses, showcasing the engineering skill and ceremonial splendor of the Qin dynasty. It was likely used as an imperial escort in the afterlife.

Civil Officer

This figure represents a civil official, characterized by calm demeanor and formal attire. It reflects the administrative structure and non-military presence within the emperor's court.

The Kneeling Archer

Depicted in a poised kneeling position, this archer is ready for battle. The figure's realism and tactical posture reflect the Qin army's discipline and strategy.

Standing Archer

A terra-cotta soldier standing with a drawn bow, symbolizing readiness and vigilance. This figure likely formed the outer ranks of the Qin archery units.

High-ranking Officer

Distinguished by elaborate armor and a commanding pose, this figure represents a senior military officer. It highlights hierarchy and leadership within the Qin army.

Middle-ranking Officer

Wearing standard military dress with modest rank insignia, this figure served as a mid-level commander, crucial for tactical execution on the battlefield.

Terra-cotta Acrobats

These figures portray entertainers such as acrobats and dancers, placed in the mausoleum to serve the emperor's leisure in the afterlife. They reflect the richness of Qin court life.

Kneeling Charioteer

This figure kneels as if holding the reins of a horse-drawn chariot. It demonstrates the realistic depiction of military logistics and support roles in the Qin army.

The Paradise of Amitabha Buddha

A vibrant depiction of the Western Pure Land, this mural portrays Amitabha Buddha surrounded by celestial musicians, bodhisattvas, and worshippers. It reflects the deep faith in rebirth and salvation.

The Thousand-Armed Avalokitesvara

This grand mural shows Avalokitesvara (Guanyin) with multiple arms, each holding a symbolic object. The image signifies boundless compassion and the power to aid countless beings.

The Nine-Colored Deer Jataka

Based on a Buddhist tale, this mural tells the story of a magical deer who saves a drowning man but is later betrayed. It conveys moral lessons of gratitude and virtue.

Emperor Wu of Liang Donating to Monks

This narrative scene illustrates Emperor Wu of Liang offering gifts to Buddhist monks. It symbolizes royal patronage and the fusion of political power with religious devotion.

Vimalakirti and Manjusri Debate

Depicting a philosophical debate between the layman Vimalakirti and Bodhisattva Manjusri, this mural explores Mahayana Buddhist thought through vivid expressions and elegant composition.

Bodhisattva Leading the Way

A Bodhisattva gracefully guides souls toward enlightenment. The mural often appears near entrances to sacred spaces, symbolizing guidance, hope, and spiritual direction.

Donor Portraits with Inscriptions

Located near cave entrances or lower walls, these images show patrons kneeling in devotion. The inscriptions record names, ranks, and wishes—valuable for historical research.

Flying Apsaras (Feitian)

Graceful celestial beings dance and play instruments in mid-air. These murals capture the joy and spiritual freedom of the Buddhist heavens, emblematic of Dunhuang art.
